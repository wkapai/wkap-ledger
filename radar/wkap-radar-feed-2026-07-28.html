THESIS_SUMMARY

VLO is not primarily a directional crude trade. It is a trade on crude
feedstock costs falling faster than gasoline and diesel prices while
effective refining capacity remains constrained.

The fundamental mechanism is credible, but Valero’s July 30 earnings must
show that theoretical crack spreads translated into realized margins,
throughput, and cash returns. (Valero Energy
<https://investorvalero.com/news/news-details/2026/Valero-Energy-Corporation-to-Announce-Second-Quarter-2026-Earnings-Results-on-July-30-2026/default.aspx?utm_source=chatgpt.com>
)
WKAP_ANGLE

The surface-level frame:

“Oil prices are falling, so energy earnings should weaken.”

The alternative frame:

“Lower crude can transfer profit from upstream producers to refiners when
product prices remain supported.”

The key research question:

“How much of the record crack spread did Valero capture after operational
outages, feedstock costs, and regional differentials?”

CORE_THESIS

A 3-2-1 crack spread approximates the value of two barrels of gasoline and
one barrel of distillate produced from three barrels of crude. It is not
refinery net profit, but it provides a useful directional measure of
theoretical refining margin. (RBN Energy
<https://rbnenergy.com/market-data/3-2-1-crack-spread>)

Using the quoted September settlements, the theoretical spread rose from
approximately $59.07 to $62.22 per barrel as crude declined faster than
gasoline and diesel.

The product market remains constrained. For the week ending July 17, U.S.
gasoline inventories were approximately 211.3 million barrels, distillate
inventories 109.6 million barrels, and refinery utilization 96.1%. Russian
diesel disruption and Middle East shipping risks have further supported
international demand for U.S. refined products. (U.S. Energy Information
Administration <https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/>)

Valero’s complexity, Gulf Coast positioning, and feedstock flexibility may
allow it to capture more of this environment than simpler refiners. The
risk is that product prices eventually follow crude lower or operational
issues prevent full capture.
