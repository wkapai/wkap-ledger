THESIS_SUMMARY

IQE is the less obvious optical-policy object because it does not sell
finished modules or raw InP substrates. It grows the epitaxial structures
that determine the performance of lasers and other photonic devices.

The fundamental thesis was strengthening before the FCC report through AI
orders, a multi-year InP agreement, and raised guidance. Today’s change is
that module onshoring is making qualified non-China epitaxy more
strategically visible.
WKAP_ANGLE

The surface-level frame:

“IQE is a secondary optical supplier that moved less than the direct module
beneficiaries.”

The alternative frame:

“IQE may be one of the few Western epitaxy platforms positioned to benefit
when module and laser supply chains require qualified, traceable, non-China
production.”

The key research question:

“Can IQE secure enough InP substrate supply to turn strategic relevance
into volume, margins, and repeat customer commitments?”

CORE_THESIS

IQE sits between raw InP substrate producers and finished laser or
optical-device manufacturers. That position matters because supplier
migration does not end when a hyperscaler replaces a Chinese module vendor.
The replacement supplier must still qualify its substrates, epitaxy,
lasers, packaging, and manufacturing flow.

The original Daily Note cited a multi-year $14 million AI and data-center
production order, a multi-year InP epiwafer agreement with Tower
Semiconductor, and FY2026 revenue-growth guidance raised to above 30%.
These claims appear to originate from company disclosures but should be
verified before external use.

The potential mispricing is that the market may treat IQE as a distant
second-order beneficiary, while qualification barriers could make epitaxy
capacity more persistent and strategic than the initial module-share trade.
