AI_FACTORY_CONTROL_PLANE_UPDATE

The control plane has five gates:

1. Demand becomes cloud and infrastructure commitments.
2. Commitments become deployed compute, networking, storage, and power.
3. Control infrastructure provisions, secures, observes, and feeds clusters.
4. Deployed clusters reach useful utilization and revenue.
5. Revenue produces margin and cash after infrastructure cost.

WKAP PATH VIEW

AI demand -> capacity commitment -> accelerator and custom-silicon
order -> network and control-plane deployment -> cluster utilization
-> token and business output -> revenue -> gross margin -> cash.

NVDA is the vertically integrated compute-and-control signal. AVGO is
the open-Ethernet and custom-silicon conversion signal. MSFT is the
buyer-operator and custom-inference return signal. The framework
compares control points; it does not assert an undisclosed supplier
relationship or assign revenue across them.

and commercial RPO reached $678 billion in Q4. Maia 200 adds
first-party inference, with a company-claimed 30% fleet
performance-per-dollar gain. The 10-K says AI-infrastructure
investment and usage pressured cloud margin. Custom silicon matters
only if it improves utilization and economics.

WKAP_ANGLE

The surface-level frame:

“Maia 200 reduces dependence on external accelerators and
automatically improves Azure margins.”

The alternative frame:

“A first-party accelerator creates an optimization lever, but
deployment, workload fit, utilization, software maturity, and total
fleet cost determine whether peak specifications become margin.”

The key research question:

“Can Maia 200 improve tokens per dollar across production workloads
quickly enough for Azure growth and RPO conversion to outrun
AI-infrastructure pressure?”

CORE_THESIS

Microsoft controls demand, scheduling, software, and a heterogeneous
fleet. Maia 200’s 10.1 PetaOPS FP4, 216 GB HBM3e, and 7 TB/s establish
architecture; the 30% performance-per-dollar improvement is
Microsoft’s claim. Maia revenue, utilization, and standalone margin
contribution are undisclosed. Azure growth, RPO conversion, cloud
margin, and fleet efficiency are the proof.
