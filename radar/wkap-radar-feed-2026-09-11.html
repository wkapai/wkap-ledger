Three market sources worth feeding into today’s market chat. Not
required reading — WKAP has already extracted the signal.

1. @stevenfiorillo — Oracle’s Hypergrowth Meets the Cash and Capital Test
URL: https://x.com/stevenfiorillo/status/2098225559293550874
WKAP signal: Steven Fiorillo frames Oracle’s capacity build as
beginning to convert into operating cash while acknowledging the
enormous capital requirement. The conversion conclusion is opinion;
Oracle’s release controls the cash facts.
Why it matters today: Attention has moved beyond backlog size to
whether cloud delivery can outrun capex and avoid recurring equity
dependence.
Themes/tickers: AI infrastructure / cloud / RPO / capex / free cash
flow / financing / ORCL
Question to ask: “Which Q2 cloud-growth, operating-cash-flow, capex,
free-cash-flow, prepayment and financing evidence would show that
Oracle’s $664 billion RPO is becoming a self-funded business rather
than a permanently funded buildout?”

2. @StockSavvyShay — TSMC’s August Throughput Creates a Measurable
September Test
URL: https://x.com/StockSavvyShay/status/2098010518934159808
WKAP signal: Shay Boloor highlights 53% year-over-year August revenue
growth and interprets ongoing capacity work as supply chasing demand.
Monthly revenue is official evidence; fab and customer conclusions
require company confirmation.
Why it matters today: TSMC offers the shortest, cleanest conversion
test because September revenue can be measured against a bounded
quarterly guide before the margin reconciliation.
Themes/tickers: Foundry / AI accelerators / monthly revenue / advanced
nodes / mix / margin / TSM
Question to ask: “Does September revenue clear roughly NT$464 billion,
and do Q3 gross margin, advanced-node mix and Q4 guidance confirm that
exceptional throughput is durable rather than timing, FX or
concentrated shipments?”

3. @thespencerwalsh — Adobe’s AI Usage Still Needs Contractual Proof
URL: https://x.com/thespencerwalsh/status/2098187600607732221
WKAP signal: Spencer Walsh calls attention to Adobe’s reported 8% RPO
growth and questions the gap between rapid freemium usage and
contractual momentum. The RPO figure is fact; cannibalization and
historical framing are interpretation.
Why it matters today: Adobe has product adoption and cash-generating
scale, but the rerating depends on whether AI engagement lifts the
much larger total ARR and backlog base through a leadership
transition.
Themes/tickers: Creative AI / freemium / Firefly / ARR / RPO / cRPO /
CEO transition / ADBE
Question to ask: “Which Q4 AI-first ARR, total ARR, paid-conversion,
Creative subscription, RPO/cRPO and margin evidence would prove that
Adobe’s free AI funnel is expanding recurring commitments rather than
substituting for them?”
